How to use this guide

  1. Work on a copy of a project, or on the bundled example examples/residual. Several steps rename, move and delete things.
  2. Tick the box beside each step you tried. The grey text says what should happen.
  3. At the end of each section, fill in three ratings. Function: Works, Rough (works but awkward), Broken, or Skipped. Feel: 1 (frustrating) to 5 (a pleasure). Matters to me: how much you would miss the feature.
  4. Under What happened, write bugs and surprises: exact error wording helps, and note the time if something crashes. Under How it felt, write your impressions and what you would change.
  5. You do not need to do every step or every section. Follow your curiosity; tick what you tried.
  6. When you are done, copy the ratings onto the scorecard on the last page.
  7. Sections 16 to 21 need AI set up (section 15). Image generation costs about $0.03 a picture.

Launching and projects 1

First impressions: getting into a project and moving around the window.

  • Start Chisel. A launch screen lists recent projects, with Open folder and New project.
  • Make a new project. Default location is your novels folder; it opens with a first scene.
  • Open an existing project with Open folder, then reopen it from the recent list.
  • Switch between two projects. Each opens where you left it; nothing from one leaks into the other.
  • Drag the window by its top bar; minimise, maximise, restore and close it.
  • Close and restart Chisel. Your last project and scene come back.
  • Resize the window narrow and wide. Panels stay usable; nothing jitters or overlaps.

Binder, scenes and parts 2

The left-hand binder: the book's structure.

  • Make a scene with Ctrl N and another from the binder's new-item button.
  • Rename a scene. The title updates in the binder, breadcrumb and window title.
  • Make a part (chapter folder), rename it, and move scenes into it.
  • Drag scenes to reorder them, inside a part and between parts. Numbering follows; the open scene stays open.
  • Move a whole part up or down.
  • Park a scene (take it out of the book without deleting it). A Parked scenes group appears; word totals drop.
  • Put the parked scene back into the book.
  • Try the Corkboard and Outline views; reorder by dragging there too.
  • Use Ctrl K to jump to a scene by typing part of its name.

Writing in the editor 3

The heart of the app. Write a few real paragraphs.

  • Type several paragraphs. The status bar shows Saved shortly after you stop.
  • Bold and italic from the toolbar and from Ctrl B / Ctrl I.
  • Undo and redo, from the toolbar and the keyboard.
  • Watch the word count for the scene, the project, and today.
  • Paste text from another program. It lands as plain text, without odd formatting.
  • Focus mode with F11, then leave it.
  • Open the scene file in another editor, change a line, come back. Chisel notices; it never silently overwrites your change.
  • Long scene: scroll, select across paragraphs, check that typing stays smooth.

Characters, places and links 4

The living story bible: names in the prose connect to notes.

  • Select a character's name in a scene and press Ctrl J to make a note. Try a place too.
  • Every later mention of the name is coloured, without brackets. Aliases count as mentions too.
  • Hover over a name. A small card shows the note's summary.
  • Click a name. The Notes tab on the right shows that note. (This used to blank the screen; it is fixed.)
  • Ctrl-click a name. The note opens in the editor.
  • Type [[Someone New]] for a name with no note. It is marked as missing; the Notes tab offers Create note.
  • Click a backlink in the Notes tab. The scene opens at that line.
  • Open a character from the binder's Characters group.

The character sheet 5

The Notes tab for one character: aliases, birth, backlinks.

  • Add an alias in the Add alias box and press Enter. The alias becomes a mention in the prose.
  • Enter a birth year in Born (e.g. 2187 or 2187-03-14). With story time on scenes, the age in this scene appears.
  • Enter nonsense in Born. "Not a story time. Kept as text." Nothing is lost.
  • Open in editor, write a description in the body, return to a scene. The Notes tab shows the new text.
  • Look at the note as it opens in the editor. Is the name / type / aliases block at the top clear, or confusing?
  • A note with no body text at all. Shows "This note has no text yet."

Relationships (new) 6

Who is whose sister, rival, boss. Written in the note; the other side is worked out for you.

  • In a character's note, add a section ## Relationships with a line - [[Other Name]] — older sister.
  • Check the Relationships list in that character's Notes tab.
  • Open the other character. The relationship appears there, marked "from their note". Their file is not changed.
  • Link to someone with no note yet. Listed with "no note yet"; no note is created.
  • (AI) Press Suggest relationships. A review list appears; nothing is saved until you accept rows.
  • Accept some suggestions, reject others. Only the accepted ones are written into the note.
  • Are the suggested labels sensible for your story?

Rename everywhere 7

Change a character's name across the whole book, safely.

  • From the Notes tab, choose Rename everywhere. A preview lists every place the name appears.
  • Untick a few occurrences, then apply. Only the ticked ones change.
  • Check the prose, the note's file name and the binder.
  • Undo the rename. Everything goes back as it was.
  • Edit a scene between preview and apply. Chisel refuses to overwrite the changed file and says so.

Scene details and story time 8

The inspector strip under the editor: status, point of view, place, purpose, and when it happens.

  • Set a status, POV character, place and chapter purpose for a scene.
  • Set a word target and watch progress toward it.
  • Set a story time (when it happens) on one scene, e.g. 2190-06. Later scenes without one inherit it.
  • Enter an invalid story time. Flagged, kept as text, never guessed.
  • Check character ages in a scene with a story time (needs Born, section 5).
  • The details never appear as text in the prose, the word count, or exports.

Spell check 9

Spelling only, offline. Never grammar.

  • Misspell a few words. They are underlined after a moment; the count shows in the status bar.
  • Put the cursor on one and press Ctrl . (or click it). Pick a suggestion.
  • Add a made-up word to the dictionary. It stops being flagged everywhere.
  • Character and place names are never flagged.
  • Open Dictionary in the binder. A plain list of your accepted words you can edit.

Settings and AI setup 15

Everything except the AI features works without this.

  • Open Settings (gear, bottom left). Look through every section.
  • Enter an OpenRouter key. Stored in the system keyring, not in the project.
  • Pick models for each role (writing, chat, checks, image). The picker lists real models with prices.
  • (Optional) Local model: with Ollama running, switch the picker to local models and choose one.
  • Run any AI action. The AI cost in the status bar goes up.
  • Start an AI request and press Stop. Nothing is inserted or saved.

Assistant chat 16

The right-hand Assistant tab. It suggests; it never edits your text.

  • Ask a question about the open scene. The About: chip shows what it is looking at.
  • Open a character, then ask about them. The chip changes to that character.
  • Remove the About chip and ask again. The answer no longer uses that item.
  • Switch scope between the current scene and the whole project.
  • Attach a scene, a note and a comment with the paperclip.
  • Open "What was sent" under a reply. Lists what was included, trimmed or left out.
  • Regenerate a reply; delete one; start a new chat; reopen an old chat from history; rename it.
  • Save a useful reply to your notebook.

Style guide and AI writing 19

The AI learns your voice, then drafts. Drafts are marked until you accept them.

  • Open Style Guide in the binder; learn the style from your prose. Edit the result.
  • Put the cursor in a scene and press Ctrl G to draft. The new text is marked as a pending draft.
  • Write {{expand: they argue about the money}} and press Ctrl G on it.
  • Select a paragraph and press Ctrl G to rewrite it.
  • Accept one draft with F7 and reject another with F8. Rejecting brings back your original exactly.
  • Does the drafted prose sound like you?

Export 23

Turn the book into something to read, send or print.

  • Export a typeset PDF book. Check the title page, contents, running heads and page numbers.
  • Export a manuscript PDF (double-spaced, line numbers).
  • Export a plain proof PDF and a Markdown file.
  • DOCX or EPUB (needs pandoc installed; greyed out without it).

Trash 24

Deleting never destroys anything until you empty the Trash.

  • Delete a scene, a notebook note and a picture.
  • Open Trash in the binder. All three are listed, each with its kind.
  • Restore each one. They return where they were, with comments and snapshots.
  • Delete the open scene. Another opens; the deleted one does not reappear.
  • Empty the Trash. Asks for confirmation first.
