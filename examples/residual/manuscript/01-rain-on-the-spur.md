# Rain on the Spur

The rain in the Hollow Market came down warm and tasted of the maglev spur
overhead: copper, ozone, and the sweet rot of the noodle stalls. Rook Tanaka
sat at the far end of Lin's counter with a clove burning down between two
fingers of his left hand. He couldn't feel it. He hadn't felt anything in
those fingers since Kessler-Voss had burned the nerves for him, three years
and one severance package ago.

"You're brooding," said Wren. Her voice came from the jack behind his ear,
pitched low, the grain of an old recording in it. "It's unattractive in a man
who owes Lin four hundred."

"I'm thinking."

"You're brooding at a speed that resembles thinking."

Lin set a glass of something green in front of him without being asked and
went back to wiping the same patch of steel she had been wiping for an hour.
Above the bar a koi holo turned slowly in the smoke, its scales flickering
through the ad rotation: soy futures, a K-V recruitment loop, a
missing-persons notice nobody would read.

His deck buzzed against his ribs. The caller ID was a row of zeros, which
meant police, which meant Kuroda.

"Don't," said Wren.

He answered.

"Tanaka." Inspector Kuroda sounded the way he always did at two in the
morning, like a man talking through a mouthful of gravel and regret. "I've got
a body at the Meridian. Capsule seven-nineteen. I need someone who can read
what the building saw before the company gets here and decides it didn't see
anything."

"Which company?"

A pause, long enough to be an answer. "Come now. Bring your ghost."

The line went dead. Rook looked at the green drink and left it where it was.

"He called me a ghost," Wren said. "I've decided to be offended later, when
it's convenient."

"Is it Kessler-Voss?"

"There are four company security flyers circling the Meridian's roof," the
construct said. "They aren't landing. They're waiting for someone to tell them
it's safe to."

Rook put the clove out on the counter, left a cred chit he couldn't spare
beside the glass, and walked out into the rain.
