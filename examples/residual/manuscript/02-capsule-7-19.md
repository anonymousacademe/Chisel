# Capsule 7-19

The Meridian stacked its sleepers forty high under the spur, a honeycomb of
fiberglass coffins lit the color of weak tea. Capsule 7-19 hung on the seventh
tier behind a sag of police tape. Somewhere below, a uniform was arguing with
the night manager about who would pay for the tape.

Rook climbed the ladder and looked in.

Imogen Sallow lay on her back with her knees drawn up, because the capsule was
too short for her. He knew the face. Dr. Sallow had given the keynote on a K-V
conference loop he'd once watched on mute: the architect behind half the ice
that guarded the company's orbital banks. Her green eyes were open, fixed on
the ceiling screen, which still ran its loop of a beach none of them would
ever see.

"No wounds," Wren said. "Her heart stopped at 03:12 Tuesday. The capsule's
medical sensors logged it, and then they logged nothing at all, because
someone told them to stop."

"Someone inside the building."

"Someone who knew the building's ice better than the building does." The
construct was quiet for a moment, reading. "Rook, the Meridian's node has been
edited. Not wiped. Wiping leaves a hole. This is a patch, stitched in with a
very fine needle."

He pulled a glove over the dead fingers of his left hand and ran it along the
underside of the mattress panel. The seam was loose. Behind it, taped flat
against the fiberglass, was a sliver of black glass no longer than his thumb.

The shard was cold. Everything in the Meridian was warm and damp, and the
shard was cold, as if it had been kept somewhere else until very recently.

"Don't," said Wren, and then, in a different voice: "No. Wait. Let me see it."

He held it up to the capsule light. For a long moment the construct said
nothing, and Rook felt her attention move through the jack like a draft under
a door.

"I know the architecture on that," she said.

"Of course you do. It's Kessler-Voss ice."

"No. I know it the way you know your own handwriting." Another pause. "She
wrote me, Rook. Sallow wrote me."

Below them the ladder creaked. Rook slid the shard into his coat pocket a
second before Kuroda's head came over the lip of the seventh tier.
