# The Stairwell

The service stairwell at the back of the Meridian smelled of bleach and old
noodles, and the light strips on every other landing had been dead so long
that someone had drawn faces on them. Kuroda caught up with him on the fourth.

"You were up there a long time." The inspector leaned on the rail. He flexed
the fingers of his right hand, the chrome one, and the old servos whined like
a mosquito in a jar. "Long enough to read a building."

"The building's been edited," Rook said. "Someone patched the Meridian's node
after she died. Clean work."

"Company work."

"You said it. I didn't."

Kuroda looked up the stairwell, toward the seventh tier and the tape and the
dead woman. Four floors above them boots were moving: soft-soled, many,
unhurried. The flyers had landed.

"As of nine minutes ago," Kuroda said, "the death of Imogen Sallow is an
internal Kessler-Voss security matter. Their people, their scene, their
coroner. My report is going to say natural causes, and it's going to say I
never called you." He paused. "Did you take anything out of that capsule,
Tanaka?"

The shard sat in Rook's pocket like a coin from another country.

"Tell him no," Wren said in his ear. "Tell him no, and mean it, and walk."

"No," said Rook.

Kuroda studied him the way he studied everything, slowly, as if the answer
might change if he waited. Then he nodded, once.

"Good. Because if you had, the company would want it back, and they'd ask
nicely exactly one time." He pushed off the rail. "Sallow checked in on
Monday night under a name that wasn't hers. Paid cash. People with K-V
salaries don't pay cash, and they don't sleep in coffins. She was hiding from
somebody."

"From the company."

"From somebody inside it." The inspector started down the stairs, and his
voice came back up the well a little flattened by concrete. "I still owe you
for Kowloon Low. Consider this me paying it off. Don't come to me again with
this one."

Rook waited until the footsteps were gone, then went down the other way.

"He knows," the construct said.

"He knows he doesn't want to know," said Rook. "That's different."
